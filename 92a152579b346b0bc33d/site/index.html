How it works

Five parts. The record is one of them.

Steel plate on the wall

Four-inch plate, levels after the screws are in, fits drywall anchors, a stud, or any existing single-gang or round box.

Hub twist-locks on

Three bayonet slots drop over the plate studs and turn twenty degrees. A set screw at six o'clock stops it ever untwisting.

Backplate carries the light

A 10.5-inch matte black disc with the dotless LED ring on its back, facing the wall, and a brass threaded insert at the centre.

Your record, then the dome

Record on the felt pads, dome through the spindle hole, finger-tight. Plug in the 12-volt adapter. Dim to taste.

The questions everyone asks

Heat, safety, wiring, renters.

Will the light heat up or warp my record?

No. The LED ring sits on the back of the backplate, facing the wall, where it cannot see the record at all. It is mounted on a thin aluminium ring that spreads its warmth, with a layer of cork and half an inch of wood between it and the record. The whole ring draws about 4 watts, and never more than 6, spread over a 28-inch circle. Vinyl starts to soften around 130 to 140 F; our heat calculation puts the record about 6 F above the room. The only way this record gets hot is if you hang it over a radiator or in direct sun, which the hang sheet tells you not to do. Our acceptance test for every batch is record face under 95 F in a 75 F room after a two-hour soak, and we publish the numbers once measured.

Is my record safe? What touches it?

Felt and one clear washer. The record rests on six felt pads and a felt ring on the backplate; a thin clear polyester washer lies on the label so the cap never turns against the paper, and the cap's own felt sits on that. The cap carries its own threaded stud, which passes through the spindle hole into a brass insert in the backplate; the smooth collar under the cap is a hair smaller than the hole, so nothing is forced and the record centres itself. You could take the record off tomorrow and play it. Three pads near the edge, three part-way in and the ring behind the label hold it flat, which is more support than a record gets in a sleeve on a shelf.

Can I change the record?

Yes, that is the point. Unscrew the dome cap, lift the record off, set the next one on, screw the cap back. Under a minute, no tools, and the fixture stays on the wall. Collectors tell us this is the whole reason they want the Kit.

How does it get power? Do I need an electrician?

No electrician. A UL-listed 12-volt adapter plugs into a normal outlet and feeds the sconce through a thin low-voltage cable. The default install hides that cable inside the wall: a 1-inch hole behind the fixture, the cable drops down the stud bay to a small jack plate at outlet height, and the adapter plugs in there. Low-voltage Class 2 cable is allowed inside walls without conduit in every US jurisdiction that follows the National Electrical Code (NEC 725). The adapter itself never goes in the wall. If you would rather not cut a hole, the surface option runs a braided cord down the wall, or inside a paintable raceway.

I rent. Can I still hang it?

Yes. The surface option needs two screw holes for the mounting plate, same as a picture. The plate also bolts to any existing single-gang or round box if you have a dead sconce location you want to reuse. The in-wall option needs a 1-inch hole and a single-gang cutout at outlet height, which is a patch job when you leave; many renters do it anyway, but check your lease.
