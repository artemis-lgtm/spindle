How it works

The wire disappears. The record stays yours.

Two install paths. In-wall is the default because that is what most people want: no cord on the wall, the adapter at the outlet where it belongs. Surface takes fifteen minutes and two screws.

The mount

A 4-inch steel plate screws to the wall over a 1-inch hole. Its arc slots match a single-gang low-voltage bracket and a round box, and take two screws into anchors or a stud when there is no box. The slots give about nine degrees of rotation, so you level the plate after the screws are started.

The hub on the back of the sconce has three J-slots that drop over the plate's bayonet studs and twist about twenty degrees to lock. One set screw at six o'clock stops a knock from ever untwisting it. The low-voltage cable comes straight through the centre of the plate into a jack in the hub.

The record is clamped from the front by the dome cap, so swapping a record never touches the mount.

Option A, the default

In-wall. About 45 minutes, no electrician.

Only the 12-volt side goes inside the wall, in cable rated CL2, which the National Electrical Code allows in walls without conduit (NEC 725). The plug-in adapter never goes in the wall: NEC 400.12 prohibits concealed power cords, so the adapter and its cord stay at the receptacle, where a wall-wart belongs.

What you see when it is done: the sconce, a white single-gang plate beside your outlet with a small DC jack in it, a 6-inch jumper, and the adapter. Nothing on the wall between.

Want the adapter hidden too? A recessed power kit (PowerBridge, Legrand, Datacomm) puts a recessed receptacle inside the wall behind a flush plate. We link to them in the hang sheet; we do not sell them.

Pick the spot

Record centre at 58 to 62 inches is typical. Stud finder: the hole must sit between studs, in the same bay as the outlet below.

Mark and cut

Plate on the wall, bubble level on its top edge, mark two slots. Cut the 1-inch hole at centre. Cut the single-gang opening beside the outlet with the bracket's template.

Pull the cable

Fish the CL2 cable from the top hole down to the bottom opening. Land the bottom end in the keystone coupler, screw the plate to the bracket.

Plate, hub, level

Screw the plate on a quarter-turn loose, level, tighten. Pass the plug through the plate into the hub jack. Hang, twist, set screw.

Record and dome

Record on the felt pads, dome through the spindle hole with its felt washer, finger-tight.

Plug in

Adapter lead into the jack plate, adapter into the outlet. Set the dimmer. Done.

In-wall kit (15 ft CL2 cable, old-work bracket, keystone plate and DC coupler, pull string, drill card) is sold as an add-on at checkout.

Option B

Surface. About 15 minutes, two screws.

Same plate, same hub. The 6-foot braided adapter lead plugs into the hub jack and leaves through the channel at six o'clock, then runs down the wall to the outlet. Add a paintable raceway if you want it flush, or let the braided cord be part of the look.

Renters: two screw holes, same as a picture frame. If you have a dead sconce box on the wall already, the plate bolts straight to it.

Option C

Hardwired, by your electrician

A listed Class 2 12-volt driver in a standard box behind the sconce, switched from the wall. Our plate bolts to the box ears. The sconce itself remains a 12-volt device with no 120-volt parts. We supply a one-page sheet for the electrician.

Heat, in numbers

Vinyl softens around 130 to 140 F. A low-power LED strip in open air runs well under that, and in this design the strip points away from the record: it sits on the backplate rim radiating outward at the wall, with air between it and the record's edge and half an inch of insulating wood between it and the record's back. Four to ten watts over a 33-inch ring has no path to lift the record more than a few degrees above the room.

Our acceptance test for every batch is a two-hour soak with an infrared thermometer at three points: strip surface, record back at the pad radius, and the label. Pass is record face under 95 F in a 75 F room. The numbers get printed here once measured.

The one real heat risk is the room, not the lamp. Do not hang any record over a radiator or in direct sun.
