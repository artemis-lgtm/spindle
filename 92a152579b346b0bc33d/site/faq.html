FAQ

Everything people ask before they reserve.

Heat, warping, record safety, wiring, renters, shipping and returns. If yours is not here, email hello@example.com.

Will the light heat up or warp my record?

No. The LED ring sits on the rim of the backplate, pointing outward at the wall, with air between it and the record's edge and half an inch of insulating wood between it and the record's back. The whole ring draws 4 to 10 watts spread over a 33-inch circle. Vinyl starts to soften around 130 to 140 F; a low-power LED strip in free air runs tens of degrees below that and the record itself sits within a few degrees of room temperature. The only way this record gets hot is if you hang it over a radiator or in direct sun, which the hang sheet tells you not to do. Our acceptance test for every batch is record face under 95 F in a 75 F room after a two-hour soak, and we publish the numbers once measured.

Is my record safe? What touches it?

Six felt pads on the backplate and a felt washer under the dome cap. That is the complete list. The cap threads onto a 1/4-20 post that passes through the spindle hole, which is wider than the post, so nothing is forced. You could take the record off tomorrow and play it. Three pads at the outer radius and three near the label hold it flat, which is more support than a record gets in a sleeve on a shelf.

Can I change the record?

Yes, that is the point. Unscrew the dome cap, lift the record off, set the next one on, screw the cap back. Under a minute, no tools, and the fixture stays on the wall. Collectors tell us this is the whole reason they want the Kit.

How does it get power? Do I need an electrician?

No electrician. A UL-listed 12-volt adapter plugs into a normal outlet and feeds the sconce through a thin low-voltage cable. The default install hides that cable inside the wall: a 1-inch hole behind the fixture, the cable drops down the stud bay to a small jack plate at outlet height, and the adapter plugs in there. Low-voltage Class 2 cable is allowed inside walls without conduit in every US jurisdiction that follows the National Electrical Code (NEC 725). The adapter itself never goes in the wall. If you would rather not cut a hole, the surface option runs a braided cord down the wall, or inside a paintable raceway.

I rent. Can I still hang it?

Yes. The surface option needs two screw holes for the mounting plate, same as a picture. The plate also bolts to any existing single-gang or round box if you have a dead sconce location you want to reuse. The in-wall option needs a 1-inch hole and a single-gang cutout at outlet height, which is a patch job when you leave; many renters do it anyway, but check your lease.

How bright is it? Can I dim it?

It is a mood light, not a reading light. Warm 2700K, high colour-rendering, dotless ring that throws an amber halo about 4 inches past the edge of the record. The inline dimmer takes it from a full glow to a candle. There is no colour-changing mode and no remote, on purpose.

Which records work?

Any standard 12-inch LP. The 7-inch singles in the Set use a chromed adapter cap that fills the large centre hole. Picture discs and coloured vinyl look spectacular with light behind them. Warped records will sit a little proud at the edge but still hang fine.

Is it UL listed?

Every electrical component is UL or ETL listed: the 12-volt adapter, the LED strip and the cable. The only parts we make are the wooden backplate, the hub, the steel plate and the cap, none of which carry current above 12 volts DC. The 120-volt side of the system lives entirely inside the listed adapter at your outlet. That is the same architecture as every LED strip kit sold in the US.
