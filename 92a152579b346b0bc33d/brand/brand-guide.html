The name: Spindle, with Deadwax as the alternate

Why Spindle

  • Only candidate clear in class 011 with a descriptive .com, a short new-TLD domain and matching handles all open at once.
  • Brand and product share one word, the Poplight pattern: Spindle 12, Spindle 7, Spindle Kit, Spindle Set. No second brand to explain on a $89 to $329 product.
  • It names our own hardware (the post through the record's hole), so it is factual about us and silent about anyone's music, which is exactly where the legal line sits.
  • Plain English object word, like Schoolhouse, Rejuvenation and Gantri names. Reads as a thing, not a startup.
  • Exposure to raise with the IP lawyer: the class 020 SPINDLE for mattresses. Different goods, same "home" aisle. File SPINDLE or SPINDLE LIGHTING on counsel's call.

Side A survives as our best tagline material ("Side A, always.") and Deadwax as the name for the editorial side if we ever want one. Neither needs to be a trademark to do that job.

02

The mark

A record seen head-on. Three groove arcs broken where the light catches them, the label at one third of the disc (4 inches of 12), and the dome at the centre where the spindle hole is. The halo is a ring behind the disc, the way the wall glows behind the real thing. Proportions are the product's proportions, so the mark is a drawing of what you get, not a metaphor.

Rules

  • Minimum size 18 px on screen, 0.35 in in print. Below that use the one-colour mark without rings.
  • Clear space equals the dome diameter times four on every side.
  • The halo ring is Halo on dark and plaster surfaces, Ember on kraft and light surfaces (Halo on cream has no contrast).
  • Never rotate, never add a second ring, never put a letter inside the disc. The one-colour mark is what gets lasered on the hub.
03

Wordmark and lockups

Inter Tight SemiBold, all caps, tracked +0.06em. A quiet grotesk plate, the kind you find stamped on Schoolhouse hardware. The horizontal lockup aligns the wordmark's x-height band to the dome centre; the stacked lockup carries a one-line descriptor in mono underneath. Alternates for Deadwax and Side A are built in the same system so a late name change costs nothing.

04

Color

Taken from the reference photograph: the black of the vinyl, the warm plaster wall, the 2700K halo. The system follows the Apple rule of one accent used sparingly, adapted warm. There is no pure white and no pure black anywhere; Label and Vinyl are the extremes. Chrome greys exist for hardware and metadata only. Brass is a configurator swatch and never a UI colour.

  • Halo is never a fill behind text. It is a ring, a gradient, a hairline, or a single highlighted word.
  • Body text on light is Vinyl; secondary text is Chrome Deep (contrast 5.6:1 on Label). On dark, Label and Chrome.
  • Finish names in the configurator are Chrome, Brass, Black, in that order, shown as rendered metal, not as flat swatches.

06

Photography

Austin's reference is the brief: a warm plaster wall at dusk, the halo doing the work, a plant edge and a frame edge in shot so it reads as a real room. Three shots per finish, in this order of importance.

Do

  • Shoot at dusk or with blackout; the halo must be the brightest thing in frame.
  • White balance to the wall, not the LED, so the glow stays amber and the plaster stays warm.
  • Second shot at 45 degrees, close on the dome reflecting the room; grooves in focus.
  • Third shot: hands unscrewing the dome and lifting the record off. That is the kit's whole pitch.
  • Plain house labels, promo blanks, or our own printed test label. The product page may show a real label as a factual example of the customer's own record.
  • One 10 to 15 second clip of the switch-on and dim to amber for Meta.

Do not

  • No band names, label logos or album art in any ad, render or social post.
  • No daylight-white LED, no RGB, no colour modes. 2700K only.
  • No white wall-wart or white cord in frame. Black cord, or the in-wall install with nothing showing.
  • No lifestyle clutter: one plant, one frame, one record. The Tom Dixon Press page is the density target.
  • No flat-lay product-on-white. The product is the glow on a wall; it has no meaning on a white sweep.
07

Voice and taglines

Plain, specific, a little dry. We write like a record store clerk who also happens to be an electrician: facts first, feelings implied. Short sentences. Numbers where they matter (2700K, 12 V, two turns of the dome). No exclamation marks, no "elevate", no "vibe", no "curated". We never say "official", "limited edition" or an artist's name. The product is the customer's record and our light; the copy knows which half is ours.

Sounds like us

  • "Your record, lit from behind. Nothing glued, nothing drilled."
  • "The dome comes off in two turns. Put on what you are playing this month."
  • "12 V from a listed adapter. The wiring goes in the wall; the light goes on the record."
  • "First batch of 20 sold out. Join the waitlist for the next one."

Does not

  • "Elevate your space with this stunning vinyl wall art!"
  • "Official [famous band] wall light" (never: trademark, copyright, and a lie about what we are)
  • "Limited edition, selling fast" for something that is made to order
  • Any sentence with "vibe", "aesthetic", or "curated" in it

08

Packaging

One kraft 13 x 13 x 3 in roll-end tuck-top mailer, printed outside in black and Ember, printed inside on the lid only. A two-layer black foam tray presents the chrome dome dead centre on the spindle axis, with the record in its sleeve safe at the bottom under everything. One uncoated 5 x 7 card with the install steps and the swap ritual, a Soot warranty card, a round seal sticker and a finish label so one box serves three finishes. Vendor-ready spec in packaging/spec-sheet.md; artwork is outlined vector with bleed, safe zones and a cut/crease legend on every sheet.

Unboxing order

  • Seal sticker off the tuck. Lid opens to "Put something on." in a halo on Soot.
  • The chrome dome, face up, alone in the centre of black foam. Card and warranty in the slot above it.
  • Layer one out: the backplate and hub in their round pocket, cord and adapter coiled beside.
  • Layer two out: the record in its poly-lined sleeve on the box floor, or the empty sleeve with a note if it is the kit.
