How we get every piece

A Spindle is about twenty small parts. None of them need inventing. Every part comes to us one of three ways.

1. We just buy itIt already exists in a store. We click, pay, and it shows up in a box. Screws, felt, the light strip, the power plug.
2. A shop makes it for usWe email a shop our drawing. They cut or shape the part and mail it back. The chrome cap, the wall plate, the black disc.
3. Not sorted yetWe know what the part is, but I have not picked who sells it or what it costs. Small cheap parts, but they are still open.

Prices are for one sconce when we order enough for 100. "Checked" means I opened the seller's page and saw the price. "Estimate" means a real quote is still needed.

What it adds up to

Parts for one 12 inch sconce come to about $38 when we buy for a hundred, or about $63 if we only build one. The two new heat rings add about $9 at a hundred ($7.19 for the metal, $1.89 for the cork), so call it $47. The three felt and plastic rings are pennies but not priced yet.

The order it happens in

  1. Order the shop parts first.The chrome cap takes about three weeks, so it sets the clock for everything.
  2. Buy the shelf parts.They show up while the shops are working.
  3. Put it together.About twenty minutes per sconce once everything is on the table.
  4. Heat test.Leave one on for two hours with a thermometer on the record. Nothing ships until that passes.
  5. Box it and send it.
