# Spindle: short-video laws and 14 shot-by-shot concepts

Written 2026-11-11 by the marketing auditor. Nothing here has been filmed or posted. This file applies the TikTok research Artemis ran for Ghost Audio (35 days, 34 daily harvest files, 4,063 unique videos, 732 unique outliers) to Spindle, and it is honest about what that research can and cannot carry.

## 1. What the research is, and its limits

- It was built for a different product (audio plug-ins) and a different audience (music producers; our own account's viewers were 84 to 91% male and mostly 18 to 34).
- The verdict on file is that it was wired in and never exercised: no video using a research hook was ever posted, and the scan was paused on 2026-10-20 (memory note project_tiktok_research_applied.md). So none of it has been proven by our own posting. It is a set of observations about other people's videos.
- "Outlier" means views at least 3 times the creator's follower count, among music-production videos with 20,000 or more views.
- I re-ran the numbers on 2026-11-11 across all 34 files. Where my figure differs from the September write-up, mine is the one used, and the sample size is stated every time.

## 2. Laws that transfer (general to short video)

| # | Law | Evidence | Sample | How Spindle uses it |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| G1 | Every video is judged cold. Followers are not the distribution. | 41% of the 732 outliers came from accounts under 5,000 followers. Our own account's traffic was 64 to 87% For You and 0 to 6% Following (tiktok-audience analytics, 9/17 and 10/12). | Large (732) plus our own account | The first frame and first line carry the whole video. Follower count is not a goal. A new account can reach people; it also means nothing compounds on its own. |
| G2 | Saves and shares mark the videos that travel. | Outliers: 26.2 saves and 4.5 shares per 1,000 views. Everything else: 13.1 and 2.1. | Large; a correlation, not a cause | Make things people keep: how to hang a record without damage, how to hide a cord, a wall-layout guide, a real temperature reading. Track saves per 1,000 views on every post. |
| G3 | Length has no reliable edge. | Outlier median ratio: 15 s and under 30.4x (n=61); 16 to 30 s 20.1x (n=165); 31 to 60 s 25.5x (n=312); over 60 s 23.0x (n=194). The share of all harvested videos that became outliers is flat by length, 41 to 49%. The September claim that "under 15 s wins" came from the first week and shrank with more data. | Medium; the short bucket is the smallest | Do not cut a video to hit 15 seconds. A one-idea demo runs 8 to 25 seconds because that is how long the idea takes. The viral one-trick plug-in demos ran 13 to 25 s (viral-plugins report, 22 examples). |
| G4 | A numbered series with a visible underdog ("Day N", "first time", "still learning") outperforms. | 9 outliers with that caption shape, median 135x against 24x for all outliers. | Small (n=9). Treat as a cheap bet, not a fact. | "Day N of building a light that holds a record." It is also the only honest content we can make before a unit exists. |
| G5 | The picture must show what the thing does before any sound or caption. | Viral-plugins report: the breakouts were plug-ins whose screen is a picture of the process ("no way this is a real plugin"); a cute interface with no process did 2.7x. | Qualitative, 22 examples | Our two processes are visible in one second each: a dark wall that blooms amber, and a dome unscrewing off a record. Open on those, mid-motion. |
| G6 | A person's hands beat a product shot. A product floating on a graded background reads as an ad and gets scrolled. | September strategy note: every sample-format outlier showed a person, a native screen or found footage; our own render-style video sat at 93 to 194 views. | Qualitative, 8 contact sheets plus our own account | Hands in every clip. No renders in organic, ever. Real wall, real room, phone camera. |
| G7 | Format can kill a good idea. | "This plugin generates sound based on the weather": 57,000 views on 53,500 followers as an 85-second talking head. | One example | Austin on camera is 15 seconds, starts on the object and not on his face, and is never the first video of the week. |
| G8 | A template other people can fill in is the biggest multiplier there is. | Delay Lama: small accounts (272 to 17,900 followers) reaching 6.2M, 2.8M, 2.2M by making their own covers with it. | One phenomenon, several videos | Let viewers choose what happens next in a way that stays clear of band names: "red label or blue label tomorrow?", "which decade?", "which wall?". |
| G9 | Making or breaking a real object on camera travels. | Destruktor 1.4M on 6,000 followers; Glitch Disks 1.5M on 16,900. | Two examples | Bench footage: cutting the cork ring, the aluminium ring going on, the strip being laid, the dome coming off the lathe if the shop allows filming. |
| G10 | Reply-to-comment is a free prompt, not a multiplier. | 9 outliers that begin "Replying to", median 28.7x, about the same as all outliers (24.2x). In the vinyl niche, @alexjbauer's reply video "here's how it was made!" has 27.2M views (yt-dlp metadata, 2026-11-11). | Small (n=9) plus one large example | Answer the heat question and the "does it damage the record" question as reply videos. Do not expect the format itself to lift views. |

Caption shape: outlier captions had a median length of 100 characters against 93 for the rest, so length is not a lever. Use one plain line, one question that invites a comment, and 3 to 5 hashtags.

## 3. Findings that do not transfer

- "Free" framing ("free plugin", "you can get it for free"): a software effect. We sell a $129 object.
- The "sample this" card, the DAW-screen formats, the FL Studio underdog arc as such: producer culture.
- Search demand ("free vst"): specific to that account.
- Any viewer demographics from the Ghost Audio account.

## 5. Rules for every Spindle video

- Real footage only. Renders never appear in organic video. If a render must appear anywhere, the word "render" is on screen.
- Every number on screen was measured on camera or comes from a datasheet on file.
- No band, artist, album or label names on screen, in captions or in replies. Records are plain-label, blank promo, or coloured vinyl with no text. A viewer asks what the record is: "a 1970s pressing with a red label".
- Sound: the real sound of the room and the hardware, or a track from TikTok's Commercial Music Library. Nothing else, because anything good becomes an ad later.
- Works with the sound off: the action plus one line of text.
- Vertical 9:16, phone, exposure locked for the glow.
- Caption: one line, one question, 3 to 5 hashtags from #vinyl #vinylcommunity #recordcollection #vinyltok #homedecor #walllight #apartmentdecor #diy #makersgonnamake #listeningroom.

## 6. The 14 concepts

Each lists the gate it needs (strategy.md section 3). Gate 0 concepts can be filmed this week if parts are on the bench; if nothing is on the bench yet, concept 1 can be filmed with a record, a pencil and a wall.

### 1. Day 1 (series opener)
- Gate: 0.
- Laws: G4 series, G6 hands, C3 founder annoyance.
- Hook line (on screen): "Day 1 of building a wall light that holds a record."
- First frame: a hand presses a bare black LP flat against a blank wall, already in motion, pencil in the other hand.
- Length: 12 to 15 s.
- Shots: (0 to 2 s) record held to the wall. (2 to 6 s) pencil traces the circle; record comes away, circle left on the paint. (6 to 11 s) the bench: MDF disc, cork ring, a coil of LED strip, laid out flat. (11 to 14 s) the strip lights for one second in the hand.
- Caption: "My records live in a crate. I want one on the wall, lit, without gluing anything to it. Day 1. What would you put up first?"
- Truth check: nothing claimed about a finished product.

### 2. The warp test
- Gate: 0, but needs a powered prototype stack and a thermocouple. This is the QC test itself, filmed.
- Laws: G2 save-worthy, G5 picture shows the process, C5 safety is the category's headline.
- Hook line: "Records warp from heat. So before anything else, we measured."
- First frame: a thermocouple taped to the back edge of a record, the meter's display readable beside it.
- Length: 20 to 25 s.
- Shots: (0 to 3 s) probe and meter, light off, starting temperature. (3 to 8 s) light on, clock on screen. (8 to 16 s) time-lapse of two hours, meter and clock both in frame the whole time. (16 to 22 s) final reading held for three seconds, room thermometer beside it.
- Caption: "Two hours on, probe on the back edge of the record. Room [X] F, record [Y] F. Full numbers in the comments."
- Truth check: post the real numbers, whatever they are. Run the stacked worst case too (warm room, light at full, two hours or more), because the product audit puts that case at the edge of where record compound softens (48 to 50 C). If the reading is over the QC limit (35 C), this video is not posted as a win; it becomes "Day N: it ran too warm, here is the fix".

### 3. The part nobody films
- Gate: 0.
- Laws: G9 making on camera, G6 hands.
- Hook line: "The part of a lamp nobody films."
- First frame: a blade already halfway through a sheet of cork along a circle template.
- Length: 15 to 20 s, real sound only.
- Shots: (0 to 4 s) cork ring cut and lifted. (4 to 9 s) aluminium ring set on the cork, four screws driven. (9 to 14 s) LED strip pressed down around the ring. (14 to 18 s) power on, ring glows on the bench.
- Caption: "Cork, aluminium, light. The cork and the metal are there to keep heat away from the record. Day 3."
- Truth check: "to keep heat away" describes the design intent; it does not claim a result.

### 4. The dome
- Gate: 0, once the first real chrome cap arrives. (This slot was "chrome, brass or black, pick one". The product audit found no real part behind the brass and black caps, so a finish poll would advertise things that do not exist.)
- Laws: G9 a made object, G6 hands, G8 let viewers choose.
- Hook line: "This one part costs more than everything else in the light."
- First frame: the chrome cap dropping into an open palm, the room reflected in it.
- Length: 8 to 12 s.
- Shots: (0 to 3 s) cap in the palm, turned in the light. (3 to 7 s) set on a plain label, finger-tightened. (7 to 10 s) the room upside down in the dome, close.
- Caption: "The dome is the only hardware you see, so it gets the money. Chrome first. What finish would you want after that?"
- Truth check: the hook is true at prototype prices (the one-off cap is about $156 of a $279 prototype, ../build/cost-model.md); re-check it against the real quote before posting. The question asks what people want; it does not offer finishes.

### 5. The switch-on
- Gate: 1.
- Laws: G5, G1 first frame.
- Hook line: none for the first second; then "Your record, lit."
- First frame: not black. A dim room with the record barely visible on the wall and a hand already on the dimmer. (A fully black first frame gives a cold viewer nothing to stop for.)
- Length: 7 to 9 s, loops.
- Shots: (0 to 1 s) hand on dimmer. (1 to 4 s) halo comes up. (4 to 7 s) slow push toward the dome. Cut so the last frame matches the first.
- Caption: "That's the whole product. A real record, a ring of warm light behind it. Would you hang one?"

### 6. The swap, timed
- Gate: 1.
- Laws: G5, C2 one number.
- Hook line: "How long does it take to change the record?" with a running stopwatch on screen.
- First frame: fingers already turning the dome, stopwatch at 0.0.
- Length: as long as the swap really takes plus 3 s.
- Shots: one unbroken take. Dome off, clear washer off, record off, new record on, washer, dome, hands away, stopwatch stops.
- Caption: "[N] seconds, one take, nothing glued. Change the record, change the room."
- Truth check: whatever N is becomes the number used everywhere. "Ten seconds" is retired until this take exists. Show what the hardware really is: the stud is part of the dome; there is no post.

### 7. "Won't it cook the record?" (reply)
- Gate: 1, after concept 2's data exists.
- Laws: G10 reply prompt, G2, C5.
- Hook line: the comment sticker itself.
- First frame: a hand flat on the back of the lit fixture next to the meter reading.
- Length: 12 to 15 s.
- Shots: (0 to 3 s) comment sticker over the lit unit. (3 to 8 s) the two-hour reading from concept 2. (8 to 13 s) the record lifted off and its edge sighted for flatness against a straightedge.
- Caption: "Fair question. Two hours on: record [Y] F in a [X] F room. The strip faces the wall on a metal ring, with cork between it and the wood."
- Truth check: no "safe", no "never". The number speaks.

### 8. You pick tomorrow's record
- Gate: 1.
- Laws: G8 template, G4 series.
- Hook line: "Red label or blue label tomorrow?"
- First frame: two plain-label records fanned in one hand in front of the lit fixture.
- Length: 8 to 12 s.
- Shots: (0 to 3 s) the two records. (3 to 9 s) yesterday's winner going on.
- Caption: "Comments decide. Red or blue?"
- Truth check: choices are colours, decades or moods, never titles.

### 9. Crate to wall
- Gate: 1.
- Laws: G5 before and after in one pass.
- Hook line: "Your best record is in a box on the floor."
- First frame: a hand flipping through a crate, already moving.
- Length: 10 to 12 s.
- Shots: (0 to 3 s) crate. (3 to 5 s) one record pulled. (5 to 9 s) match cut: the same record on the wall, light coming up. (9 to 11 s) wide.
- Caption: "It took me ten years to put one on the wall. Which one would you rescue?"

### 10. Install, real time
- Gate: 1.
- Laws: C2 one number, G2 save-worthy.
- Hook line: "Installing a wall light with no electrician. Clock's running."
- First frame: the steel plate against the wall, level on top, drill in hand, timer at 0:00.
- Length: 25 to 40 s (speed-ramped, timer always visible).
- Shots: plate, two screws, hub twists on, set screw, cord down to the outlet, record on, dome on, switch on.
- Caption: "[N] minutes start to finish with the cord down the wall. Renters: it comes off when you move."
- Truth check: N is the real time. "Fifteen minutes" is retired until this exists.

### 11. The corner at dusk
- Gate: 1.
- Laws: niche format (the "finally built my vinyl corner" genre in the reference table), G6.
- Hook line: "The corner is done."
- First frame: wide shot, chair, turntable, the lit record as the only light, a hand lowering the needle.
- Length: 10 to 14 s.
- Shots: (0 to 4 s) wide. (4 to 9 s) slow push on the halo. (9 to 13 s) dimmer down.
- Caption: "Chair, turntable, one light. What's missing?"
- Truth check: the reference videos for this genre were not re-opened in this audit.

### 12. What went wrong
- Gate: 0 or 1.
- Laws: G4 underdog, C3.
- Hook line: "Three things I got wrong building this."
- First frame: a hand slapping the old drawing (light on the rim) down on the bench, a red pen already crossing it out.
- Length: 15 to 20 s.
- Shots: three mistakes, three seconds each, one line of text each, each shown with the real drawing or the real part; then the current version.
- Caption: "On paper the light sat on the rim. The heat numbers said that was too close to the record, so it moved to the back before I built one. Day N."
- Truth check: use only mistakes that happened. The rim-to-back move is real and was caught in a calculation, not on a built unit (../cad/FIXES-heat-layout.md); say it that way. Do not stage a scorched part. This is the strongest trust signal we have.

### 13. Unit number 1
- Gate: 2 (needs the ledger of the 20 and a ship week).
- Laws: G7 short founder clip, C3.
- Hook line (Austin, spoken, object in frame first): "Twenty people paid for this before one existed. This is number one."
- First frame: the finished unit on the bench with a paper tag "1 of 20", Austin's hand on it.
- Length: 15 s.
- Shots: (0 to 4 s) the tagged unit. (4 to 10 s) Austin, one sentence on why. (10 to 15 s) it lights.
- Caption: "The first 20 are spoken for. Batch two ships the week of [SHIP WEEK]. Link in bio."
- Truth check: runs only if the ledger shows the 20 paid, and only with a real ship week.

### 14. The first wall that isn't mine
- Gate: 3.
- Laws: C4 customer photos, C6 proof.
- Hook line: "The first one on a wall I don't own."
- First frame: a customer's photo or clip, used with written permission.
- Length: 8 to 12 s.
- Shots: the customer's wall; their one-line comment as text.
- Caption: "From [first name], [city]. Yours?"
- Truth check: written permission; any real label blurred or cropped; if the customer was given anything for it, that is disclosed on screen.

## 7. Posting order

Gate 0: concepts 1, 3, 12, 2, then 4 when the first cap exists; three to five posts a week, numbered as days.
Gate 1: 5, 6, 9, 10, 7, 8, 11, then repeat 6 and 8 as the running series.
Gate 2: 13.
Gate 3: 14, then every customer wall that comes in.

Judge a format after ten posts, not one (the September strategy's own rule). Log views at 48 hours and 7 days, saves per 1,000 views and comments for every post in a simple sheet.
