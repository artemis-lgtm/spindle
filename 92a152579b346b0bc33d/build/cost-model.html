3. What the numbers say

  1. The $89 Kit does not survive paid acquisition. At qty 100 it clears $25 before ads, so it breaks even at a $25 CPA and loses $14 at the $39 median. The engineering report said $79 was thin at a $27 BOM; the mounting decisions (steel plate, bayonet, set screw, bonded chrome dome, separate dimmer and jack) moved the BOM to $38 and the Kit is now thin at $89. Options, for Austin, not decided here: (a) $99 to $109 Kit; (b) sell the Kit only as an upsell/organic SKU and never put ad spend behind it; (c) drop the Kit to the keyhole mount and the Outwater flat cap ($38 to about $30 at 100, contribution about $33); (d) make the Kit the second unit in a cart (CPA is paid once per order).

  2. The Sconce at $129 works at the median. $58 contribution at 100 units, $19 left after a $39 CPA, break-even at $58. Below the scope report's $79 because the real BOM is $38 not $20 and shipping is $14.65 not $14 all-in. It fails at the $60 scenario by $2, so the $80 kill line in scope-report.md is too generous for this SKU; the honest kill line for the Sconce is $55.

  3. The named title at $179 absorbs a $60 CPA ($18 left) and breaks even at $78. The $50 price step covers a $28 record and leaves $20 more contribution. The upsell menu is the ad-math cushion, as scope-report.md said.

  4. The Set is where the money is. One CPA, three units, one box: $108 (kit) to $172 (with records) contribution at 100, break-even CPA $108 and $172. Even at an $80 CPA the Set with records nets $92. If ads run against one hero, it should be the Set, with the single Sconce as the down-sell on the page.

  5. Shipping is 11% of the Sconce price and 16% of the Kit price. Cubic pricing is already the best USPS case; the only further lever is a 12 x 12 x 3 box (432 cu in, 0.25 cu ft, still the 0.30 tier, no saving) or regional fulfilment, which is a 500-unit problem.

  6. Qty 1 to 20 is a loss or near-loss on the Kit and only $31 to $42 on the Sconce. The first 20 already-sold units (NOTES.md) should be built from the qty-20 tier and treated as the paid prototype run, not as a margin sample.

Against it: 100 deposits at $25 = $2,500 in hand before parts are ordered; balance payments ($104 per vintage Sconce, $154 named, $64 Kit) arrive as units ship, so the shipping line is self-funding. Revenue on the mix: 60 x $129 + 10 x $179 + 30 x $89 = $12,200; landed cost on the mix about $6,500; Stripe about $340; contribution about $5,350 before ads. At a $39 CPA for 100 orders ($3,900 ad spend), the batch nets about $1,450. At $60 it nets a loss of about $650. At $25 it nets about $2,850. That is why the Set and the named title have to be in the mix: with 20 of the 100 orders being Sets with records instead of Kits, batch contribution rises by about $2,900.

5. Sensitivities worth knowing

  • Every $1 of BOM is $100 on the batch and $1 of break-even CPA on every SKU.
  • Adapter at a real $7 instead of the $8.50 estimate: +$1.50 per unit. Ask LED Updates for a 100 price.
  • Steel plate powder-coated vs bare laser: about $2.50 per unit. Zinc at a local plater with a lot minimum is about $1 at 100.
  • One adapter for the Set's three units (Y-lead; a design change, not assumed): saves about $14 per Set at 100, so Set contribution goes to about $122 / $186.
  • Record at $5 instead of $2 (if the craft lots are 40% unusable, scope-report.md risk 5): -$3 on every vintage Sconce.
  • Shipping zone mix is an estimate; if the first 20 buyers are mostly Florida and the Southeast, the blended label drops to about $10.
