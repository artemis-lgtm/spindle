## 1. What is selling in wall sconces right now

The market splits into three bands that barely overlap, and the gap between them is where we sit. The mass band is Amazon and Wayfair: sets of two plug-in sconces at $30 to $80, brass or black, fabric or glass shade, with a dimmer knob and a 6 ft cord. The Yunaking plug-in pair is the archetype (4.4 stars, about 506 reviews, "500+ bought in past month" per the snippet; price not confirmed on page, estimate $35 to $65 for two) and the thing its reviews praise is stepless colour-temperature adjustment at that price (https://www.amazon.com/wall-sconce-plug/s?k=wall+sconce+plug+in, https://homebeautifuldesign.com/best-plug-in-wall-sconce/). VATONI's dimmable swing-arm plug-in runs $34.99 for a 2-pack up to $46 single on eBay and Lightsdaddy (https://www.amazon.com/VATONI-Sconces-Dimmable-Bathroom-Included/dp/B0C49MDD3M, https://www.ebay.com/itm/155149819560). Wayfair's top-rated list is the same shape: a 2-piece metal sconce set at $39.99 (4.6 stars), an iron plug-in/hardwire armed sconce at $79.99 (4.7), and at the top a Zolee glass-and-brass pair at $329.99 "was $999" (4.8) (https://www.wayfair.com/lighting/sb0/wall-sconces-c416508.html). What sells here is a dimmer, warm light, a set of two, brass or black, and no electrician. The reviews complain about thin metal, cool-white 6000K modes, remotes, and visible cords.

The viral mid band is Poplight at $99: rechargeable, adhesive-mounted, 300 lumens, seven shade colours, built-in leveller, app dimming, 5 to 9 hours per charge. ## 2. Design trends 2025 to 2026 and what separates cheap from premium

Finishes. Brass still leads catalogue lighting, but it has moved from polished gold to brushed, champagne and aged (https://www.idealhome.co.uk/all-rooms/lighting-trends-2026, https://nauradika.com/blogs/news/colour-trends-in-lighting-brass-vs-black-vs-chrome-what-designers-are-choosing-in-2026-2027). Matte black is the contrast finish that "reads as a line against any wall" (https://lightfixturesusa.com/blogs/lighting-trends/2026-lighting-trends-what-americas-top-interior-designers-are-actually-specifying). Chrome is the contested one: trade blogs say it has "retreated" from mainstream spec, while Elle Decor named chrome and silver among the comebacks shaping 2026, and the chrome-lighting guides agree that what makes the comeback work is warm 2700K LED behind it and matte textures around it, never daylight white and never a second shiny metal in the same object (https://www.aol.com/articles/6-design-trends-making-comeback-153400026.html, https://www.hausgem.com/blogs/news/modern-chrome-wall-lights-buying-guide, https://oblist.com/blogs/editorial/chrome-and-metal-the-futuristic-decor-trend-taking-over-2026-chrome-decor). For us that lands cleanly: a mirror-chrome dome against a matte black vinyl disc and a warm amber halo is the exact reflective-on-matte pairing those guides describe, and the Flos Foglio chrome-at-double-price datapoint says chrome reads as the premium version, not the cheap one, as long as the metal is real and the light is warm. Brass is the safe second finish for the catalogue-brass customer; black is the "whole object disappears into the wall, only the halo shows" option.

Halo and backlit discs. Backlit mirrors, halo channel letters and disc sconces (Lodes Puzzle, RBW Pastille, Tom Dixon Press, the Etsy halo discs) are all the same move: a flat object floating an inch off the wall with light leaking around the edge. Houzz's 2026 list files this under "soft, ambient illumination" and "integrated lighting" (https://www.houzz.com/magazine/8-lighting-trends-to-look-out-for-in-2026-stsetivw-vs~183696378). Nobody in that category uses a found object as the disc. A real LP has what pressed glass is imitating: concentric grooves that catch light.

Plug-in for renters. Poplight, Wooj, Schoolhouse's plug-in Isaac and the whole Amazon top shelf say the same thing: no electrician, a dimmer in hand, a cord that looks intentional. "Plug-in wall sconces have become a favorite solution for renters and homeowners" and "in 2026 the look is everywhere" (https://onehometherapy.com/plug-in-wall-sconces-that-add-warm-glow-without-an-electrician, https://therentaltech.com/7-wall-lights-for-renters/).

Warm-dim. The quiet 2026 shift is dim-to-warm: 2700K at full, sliding toward 2200K to 1800K candlelight as you dim, the hotel-bar effect, done with a warm-white plus amber chip blend (https://lightfixturesusa.com/blogs/lighting-trends/bathroom-lighting-trends-2026-the-backlit-mirror-replaces-the-vanity-bar, https://www.focalpointlights.com/warmdim, https://dulora.com/collections/dim-to-warm). Dim-to-warm COB tape exists (Gladiator "Valent" warm-dim tape, https://gladiatorlighting.com/collections/valent-warm-dim) but needs a two-channel driver, so it is a batch-two upgrade, not batch one. Austin's reference image reads warmer than 2700K on the wall, closer to 2200K amber (estimate from the photo), which is a point for 2400K to 2700K strip rather than 3000K.

"Jewellery" hardware. Designers describe 2026 fixtures as "wall jewelry": one highly detailed focal fixture, the rest of the room supporting it; agate-in-brass, leather-wrapped arms, marble bases, tactile weight (https://www.verityrank.com/blog/wall-sconce-lighting-guide-guide, https://electronics.alibaba.com/buyingguides/cool-sconces-guide-cordless,-sculptural-colorful-picks). The Press dome is jewellery: one polished part on a matte body. Our cap is the only piece of metal the customer sees and touches, so it carries the whole premium signal.

What reads cheap versus premium, from the review complaints across the table: cheap is thin stamped metal, hollow plastic, ABS "chrome" plating that scratches to grey, visible screws and seams, individual LED dots, RGB colour-changing modes and remotes, 6000K cool white, acrylic that scratches and shows fingerprints, printed slogans ("NOW PLAYING"), and a white wall-wart cord taped to the wall. Premium is weight in the hand, a single consistent finish, dotless warm light with a real dimmer, a cord that looks chosen (braided, colour-matched), a flush wall gap, a cap that turns with a satisfying thread, no logos, and packaging that protects a design purchase ("a dented box or broken diffuser signals poor quality control", https://thelights.shop/why-lighting-brands-should-rethink-packaging-lessons-from-th).

## 3. Vinyl and music decor: what sells and what the reviews hate

The "Now Playing" stand is the volume product: wooden base, two acrylic sheets, USB LED bar, often a 7-colour remote; $18.99 (ZEDKEHO) to $65.99 (YuanDian), YuanDian at 4.4 stars from thousands of reviews (https://www.shuttergroove.com/reviews/hi-fi/best-now-playing-vinyl-record-stands/, https://www.billboard.com/culture/product-recommendations/light-up-vinyl-record-stand-now-playing-sign-buy-online-1235950991/). The complaints: "build quality feels lighter than expected", "poorly made", acrylic scratches and cracks, external USB cable needed, lights the sign rather than the record (https://www.aliexpress.us/item/3256810330068516.html?gatewayAdapt=glo2usa4itemAdapt, https://mancaveinsider.com/decor-accessories/best-led-backlit-vinyl-frames-to-showcase-your-records). They read as a gadget on a shelf, not a fixture on a wall, which is the kit's whole argument.

Record wall display is dominated by $13 to $35 multipacks of acrylic or wood ledges (upsimples 8-pack, 87% five-star; NIUBEE 6-pack; 12-pack at $12.99) (https://pctechkits.com/best-vinyl-display-shelves/, https://www.amazon.com/vinyl-record-display-wall-mount/s?k=vinyl+record+display+wall+mount). Complaints: shallow ledges, acrylic shows scratches and fingerprints, lightweight mounts, "two of my double record frames fell, putting glass everywhere" (Record Roots customer on a competitor frame, https://record-roots.com/products/record-roots-frame). Art Vinyl's Play & Display flip frame ($45 to $52) is the one with no complaints in its reviews: one screw, front-loading, swap without taking it down, four rubber pads keep it flush (https://www.bestbuy.com/site/reviews/art-vinyl-wall-hanging-display-for-most-12-vinyl-records-black/6851051, https://artvinyl.com/product/art-vinyl-play-and-display-12-record-frame-black/). The backlit-frame roundup lists the LED-frame pain points we must not inherit: frames warp after long hangs, the record rotates if not seated, Govee whites "skew cooler than true warm", adhesive fails on textured walls, and the CLRCASE acrylic has a "plasticky feel" and smudges (https://mancaveinsider.com/decor-accessories/best-led-backlit-vinyl-frames-to-showcase-your-records).

Vinyl lamps on Etsy are melted, drilled or glued records (stacked-LP pendants CA$146, 5-tier lamp $80, single-record pendants $22 to $30) plus turntable accessory lights with mediocre ratings (mingoaudiovisual's rechargeable dust-cover lights at 3.5 and 3.7 stars, complaint: the light does not always come on) (https://www.etsy.com/market/vinyl_record_lamp, https://www.etsy.com/listing/606343271/rechargeable-led-light-for-vinyl-record). The $20.89 resin "Record Store Retro Wall Light" on Walmart and Amazon is a 5.9 x 7 inch 3xAAA toy and is the price anchor that makes anything under $70 look like a novelty (https://www.walmart.com/ip/Record-Store-Retro-Wall-Light-Vintage-Vinyl-Record-Wall-Decor-Music-Lover-Gift-Retro-Room-Aesthetic-Record-Collector-Gift/16764366840). LED vinyl wall clocks ($25 to $60, RGB remotes, laser-cut records) share the RGB-remote cheapness and most are AC-powered light plus battery clock, two power systems in one object (https://www.amazon.com/Music-Vinyl-Record-Backlight-Design/dp/B0GMCB5RRD).

What to avoid, in one list: RGB and colour-changing modes, remotes, cool white, visible LED dots, acrylic, adhesive-only mounting, a record that can rotate or warp on the mount, lighting the hardware instead of the record, and any design that damages the record (heat, glue, drilling). What to take: Art Vinyl's one-screw self-levelling hang and swap-without-removing, the Now Playing stand's "show what you're listening to" story, the frames' halo effect done properly with dotless COB.

## 4. Design recommendation

Finish options and the lead. Lead with polished chrome, as Austin decided. It is the Press reference, it is the finish that doubles Flos's price, and chrome-on-matte-black-vinyl with a warm halo is the exact "reflective against matte, warm light only" recipe the chrome-revival guides prescribe. The cap must be real metal (polished stainless or chrome-plated solid brass, not plated ABS) because a plated plastic dome scratching to grey is the single fastest way to look like the $20 toy. Second finish: brushed brass, for the catalogue-brass buyer and for anyone whose room is already West Elm brass; brushed hides fingerprints where the Press's polished gold shows them. Third: gloss black, the stealth version where the whole fixture disappears and only the halo and a black jewel show. Do not offer more than three at launch; Gantri and Poplight sell colour, but their object is the shade, and ours is the record. One cap finish per order, with the cap sold separately at $19 to $24 later as a swap (estimate; Press ships all three caps, we should not at $129).

Cap size and proportion. The LP label is 4" (100mm) across and the spindle hole is 0.286". On the Press, the dome is roughly a fifth of the 13.8" disc, about 3" (estimate measured from the DWR image). A 3" dome on a 4" label would bury the label, and the label is the reason the customer bought the record, so the right answer is a dome that covers the worn spindle-hole ring and nothing else: 1.25" to 1.5" diameter (32 to 38mm), 0.35" to 0.45" tall, a low segment-of-sphere rather than a hemisphere, with a soft radius at the base so it sits like a droplet on the label. That is 30 to 38% of the label and about 12% of the record, big enough to read as jewellery from across the room, small enough to leave the label legible. The engineering report's 1" Rockler knob is the floor; render 1.25" and 1.5" and pick on the label. For 7" singles, which have a 1.5" hole, the cap must cover the snap-in adapter, so it needs to be about 1.9" to 2.0" minimum; a chrome dome that size on a 3.5" 45 label is heavy, so the better move is to make the 7" cap literally a chromed 45 adapter shape (the vintage spider or the plain ring) in the same finish, 1.75" to 2.0", which collectors recognise instantly and which turns a constraint into a reference. One stud size (1/4-20) for both.

Halo colour temperature. 2700K COB, CRI 90+, as the engineering report specifies, with an inline dimmer; Austin's reference reads closer to 2200K amber on a cream wall, so if the supplier has 2400K, render both and pick against a warm-white and a cool-grey wall. Never above 3000K; never tunable or RGB in this product. Dim-to-warm (2700K to 1800K) is the premium upgrade for batch two and is worth a line on the page as "coming", because it is the one spec the trend press is calling the quiet shift of 2026.

Cord. The cord is visible on every plug-in sconce and the cheap ones ignore it. Ship a 6 ft braided cotton cord with a matching inline rotary dimmer: black braid with chrome and gloss black caps, oatmeal or natural braid with brass (estimate on supply: 12V DC braided cable with 5.5x2.1 barrels is a custom-overbraid job, Color Cord Company and similar sell braided 18/2 by the foot, https://www.colorcord.com/blogs/lighting-design-blog/2026-lighting-fixture-trends-what-s-in-and-out). The cord exits at 6 o'clock from the hub and drops straight; the adapter's own lead should be black, not white, and the wall-wart should be black. Offer the paintable cord channel as a $15 add-on, exactly as the engineering report says.

Packaging and unboxing. One kraft 13x13x3 double-wall mailer with a die-cut foam or moulded pulp tray; the record (if included) in a black poly-lined inner sleeve in its own slot; the cap in a small black drawstring bag or a tiny rigid box sitting in the centre of the tray where the spindle hole would be, so the first thing seen on opening is the chrome dome; the cord coiled under the tray; a single uncoated card with the hang-and-swap steps on one side and the safety and heat note on the other; a 45 adapter taped to the card. Premium unboxing costs about $3 to $8 per order and 40% of shoppers share unboxing content, which for a product that photographs this well is free advertising (https://www.shipbob.com/blog/unboxing-experience/, https://crateform.com/blog/unboxing-experience-guide). No tissue paper, no stickers with band names, no "Now Playing" slogans.

Naming. The product is the cap plus the halo, and the cap is a spindle, so the name should come from the turntable, not from any artist. Candidates: Spindle (product name: "Spindle Sconce", "Spindle Kit"), Deadwax (the run-out groove; insider term, strong brand name, unused in lighting as far as I can see, estimate), Runout, Side A, Lathe, Pressing, Halo 33, Orbit, Revolve. Recommendation: brand "Deadwax" or "Side A" as the company, "Spindle" as the sconce, "Spindle Kit" as the light-only SKU, finishes as "Chrome", "Brass", "Black", and the trio set as "The Set" or "Single and Album". Avoid "Halo" (saturated across Etsy and Amazon), "Vinyl" (Diesel/Lodes already own it in lighting), and anything with "Record Store". Trademark-search the final pick before the page goes live.

Photography for ads. Austin's reference is the brief: warm plaster wall, dusk interior, the halo doing the work, a plant edge and a frame edge in shot so it reads as a real room. Three shots per finish: (1) the hero, straight on, 12" centre with two 7"s, all three lit, room lights down so the halo is the only light source, exposure for the wall glow not the record; (2) the detail, 45 degrees, close on the chrome dome reflecting the room, the label and groove texture in focus; (3) the swap, hands unscrewing the cap and lifting the record off, which is the kit's whole pitch in one frame. Use records whose labels carry no recognisable artist or logo for every ad (plain-colour house labels, promo blanks, or our own printed test label on a blank), since no band names or logos is the rule; the product page may show a real label as a factual example of what the customer's record will look like, per scope-report.md section 4. Shoot a 10 to 15 second clip of the sconce switching on and dimming to amber, which is the Meta creative the scope report already calls for.

Price ladder. Spindle Kit (light-only, customer's record): $89, any finish, with brass and black at the same price so the finish choice is about the room, not the wallet; the engineering BOM says $79 leaves about $34 contribution after shipping and processing and does not survive a $39 CPA, and nothing credible in the halo-disc category sells under $85 anyway. Spindle Sconce (our record, "your decade, our pick"): $129 as Austin set, which sits exactly between the Etsy halo discs ($85 to $164) and the catalogue singles ($149 to $299) with a real LP the competitors do not have. Named-title Sconce: $179, same as planned, menu of ten over-pressed classics in body copy only. The Set (12" sconce plus two 7" kits with chromed 45-adapter caps, one adapter and cord per unit): $249 kit-only, $329 with our records; BOM estimate for three units is about $27 for the 12" plus roughly $20 each for the 7"s (smaller plate, same hub, adapter, cap) so about $67 parts plus about $20 shipping for a 14x14x5 box, which leaves roughly $150 contribution kit-only before ads (estimate). Caps as a later accessory at $19 to $24. Hold the floor at $89; the $20.89 resin light and the $27 Now Playing stand make anything below that read as a toy, and the $755 Press and $740 Vinyl make $129 read as a steal.

Variants worth rendering next (five, plus one stretch):

1. Spindle Chrome (default). 12" LP, 1.5" mirror-chrome low dome with soft base radius, 10.5" matte black backplate, 1" wall gap, 2700K CRI 90 COB rim-mounted outward, black braided cord exiting at 6 o'clock, black inline dimmer. Render on warm cream plaster and on a dark green wall.
2. Spindle Brass. Same geometry, 1.25" brushed brass dome, 2400K strip if available (else 2700K), oatmeal braided cord. Render beside a West Elm-style brass table lamp to check it sits in a brass room.
3. Spindle Black. Gloss black dome, matte black plate, black cord, 2700K. Render on a white wall where the halo is the only visible light and on a black wall where only the dome shows.
4. Floating double halo. The engineering report's standoff configuration: record 1" forward of the plate on the 3/8" standoff, so light leaks both behind the record (onto the plate face) and behind the plate (onto the wall), two concentric halos. Chrome cap. This is the one that looks most like Press and the one most likely to justify a $149 to $169 sconce price; render and judge against variant 1 for whether the second ring reads as richer or busier.
5. The Set. Variant 1 centre plus two 7" singles on 6" backplates with 1.9" chromed 45-adapter caps, 24" apart, the reference-image composition. Render straight on and at 30 degrees so the three halos overlap on the wall.
6. Stretch: Ring variant. A 1/4" frosted acrylic ring visible around the record edge, lit from the rim, so the halo has a glowing edge on the object as well as on the wall (closer to Press's lit glass). Adds a part and some cost; render only if 1 and 4 both feel flat.

Each render should come as the three ad shots above (hero, dome detail, swap) so the renders double as the first landing-page images and the choice is made on how it sells, not just how it looks.
