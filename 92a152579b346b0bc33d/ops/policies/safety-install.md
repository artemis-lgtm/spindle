## THE CARD (one page, both sides, goes in every box)

### Front: Read this first

**[BRAND] [MODEL]. Indoor wall light. 12 V DC, Class 2. Dry locations only.**

Use only the power adapter that came in this box (or an identical replacement from us). It is a UL Listed Class 2 12 V DC adapter. Plugging in anything else, including a 24 V adapter or a charger with a different plug, can overheat the light and void the warranty.

**Do**
- Hang it indoors on a dry wall. Living room, bedroom, hallway, studio, office.
- Keep the adapter plugged into a wall outlet or a power strip, in the open, where you can reach it.
- Dust it with a dry cloth, unplugged.
- Swap records as often as you like: unscrew the dome, lift the record off, set the new one on the felt pads, screw the dome back finger-tight.

**Don't**
- Don't hang it in a bathroom, kitchen splash zone, laundry, sauna, covered porch, or anywhere it can get wet or steamy.
- Don't hang it directly above a radiator, heater, stove, or fireplace, or where afternoon sun hits the record. Heat warps vinyl long before it bothers the light.
- Don't put the power adapter or its cord inside a wall, ceiling, or floor. Ever. (If you want hidden wiring, see "In-wall wiring" on the back; only the thin low-voltage cable goes in the wall.)
- Don't cover the adapter with a rug, cushion, or curtain.
- Don't open the hub, cut the LED ring, or modify the wiring.
- Don't let children hang on it. It holds a record, not a person.

**If something seems wrong.** Flicker, a buzzing adapter, a warm smell, or a cracked part: unplug it and email [SUPPORT EMAIL]. We will sort it out.

### Back: Hanging it

Tools: pencil, level, drill with 1/8" bit (and a 1" hole saw for in-wall), Phillips screwdriver, the little L-key in the bag.

**Option A: Surface cord (15 minutes, no holes but two screws)**
1. Pick the spot. The centre of the record usually looks right at 58 to 62" from the floor. Make sure the 6 ft adapter cord reaches an outlet.
2. Hold the steel plate on the wall, bubble level on its top edge, and mark two opposite slots.
3. Drill 1/8" pilot holes. If you hit a stud, drive the #8 screws straight in. If not, push in the supplied drywall anchors first, then the screws. Leave them a quarter turn loose.
4. Level the plate (the slots let it rotate), then tighten.
5. Plug the adapter's cord into the jack on the back of the hub and lay the cord in the channel at the bottom of the hub.
6. Line the three slots on the hub up with the three studs on the plate, push flat, twist clockwise until it stops. Turn the small set screw at the bottom in with the L-key until snug. It is now locked on.
7. Run the cord down to the outlet. A stick-on paintable cord cover (hardware store, about $10) hides it.
8. Set your record on the three felt pads, drop the dome cap through the centre hole with its felt washer on, turn until finger-tight. Plug in. Dim to taste.

**Option B: In-wall wiring (about 45 minutes, uses the optional In-Wall Kit)**
Only the thin low-voltage cable goes inside the wall. The power adapter stays in the room, plugged into your outlet. Read the whole In-Wall Kit sheet before cutting anything.
1. Steps 1 and 2 above, but choose a spot between two studs, in the same bay as an outlet below it. Use a stud finder.
2. Cut a 1" hole at the centre mark. Cut the single-gang opening for the low-voltage bracket 12 to 18" above the floor, beside the outlet, using the bracket's template. Clip the bracket in.
3. Drop the cable from the top hole to the bottom opening (barrel plug end stays at the top). Snap the DC coupler into the keystone plate, land the bottom end of the cable in it, screw the plate to the bracket.
4. Screw on the steel plate (steps 3 and 4 above). Pass the cable's barrel plug through the plate's centre hole into the hub jack. Hang and lock the hub (step 6 above).
5. Plug the adapter's short lead into the coupler on the wall plate and the adapter into the outlet. Record on, dome on, plug in.

**A note on wiring and your local code.** The In-Wall Kit is a low-voltage Class 2 cable rated for use inside walls (marked CL2). In the United States, the National Electrical Code (Article 725) allows Class 2 low-voltage cable like this to run inside walls without conduit, and most places let a homeowner install it without a permit or an electrician. Local rules vary and some cities or landlords require a permit or a licensed installer for any in-wall work, so check with your local building department or your landlord before you cut. Never run the power adapter, its cord, or any 120 V wiring inside a wall; that is prohibited by code (NEC 400.12) and is a fire risk. If you are unsure, hire a licensed electrician; it is a 30-minute job for them.

**Hardwired installs.** If an electrician wants to feed the sconce from a switched circuit with a Class 2 LED driver in a box behind it, give them the installer sheet at [SITE URL]/install. The sconce itself stays a 12 V DC device; the driver must be a listed Class 2 12 V DC unit (20 W class) with a 5.5 x 2.1 mm plug, centre positive.

**Specs.** Input to hub: 12 V DC, 2 A max, 5.5 x 2.1 mm, centre positive. Light: about 9 W, 2700 K warm white, dimmable. Weight with record about 1.5 lb. Plate fits a single-gang low-voltage bracket, a round or octagon box, or two anchors.

Why each item: the input rating and polarity so a lost adapter is replaced correctly; "Class 2 power unit" is the phrase the NEC and UL 2108 use for the supply this device is designed for; "dry locations" is the standard luminaire location marking (UL 1598 uses "dry", "damp", "wet"); the manufacturer name and date code are what the CPSC and any insurer will look for first if a unit ever comes back.
