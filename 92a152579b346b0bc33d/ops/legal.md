## 1. Records, band names, album art

The position: we sell our own product (Spindle) built around a genuine used record. The record is material, described factually. That is the Hamilton v. Vortic fact pattern (2d Cir. 2021, https://law.justia.com/cases/federal/appellate-courts/ca2/20-3369/20-3369-2021-09-14.html), where a maker won by disclosing that genuine branded parts were reused and never implying endorsement.

The line we do not cross is Chanel v. Shiver & Duke (https://www.thefashionlaw.com/chanel-prevails-in-latest-round-of-trademark-lawsuit-over-upcycled-buttons/): the other party's mark becomes the selling point of our product.

Rules, applied everywhere (site, ads, renders, emails, packaging):
- Product title is always ours: "Spindle 12", "Spindle Set". Never "Led Zeppelin Sconce", never "Official", never "Limited Edition <band>".
- Album and artist names appear only in body copy, as a fact about the material, on the Named tier only.
- We never reproduce cover art, logos or band marks as our own graphic, sticker or print. Photos of the actual product with its real label are fine (17 U.S.C. 113(c), https://www.law.cornell.edu/uscode/text/17/113).
- No artist name or likeness as an ad headline. Florida's right-of-publicity statute (Fla. Stat. 540.08, http://www.leg.state.fl.us/statutes/index.cfm?App_mode=Display_Statute&URL=0500-0599/0540/Sections/0540.08.html) bites when a name promotes a product.
- Non-affiliation line on every product page and in terms (already in site/terms.html and about.html).
- Two-business-day takedown: if a rights holder asks, we stop offering that title. No fight. (Already in terms.)

## 2. "Made from an original pressing" wording

Safe: "Spindle 12 Named. Built around an original 1977 pressing of Rumours by Fleetwood Mac. Genuine used LP; label wear is part of the piece. Not affiliated with, sponsored or endorsed by the artist or label."

Risky: the album as the product name, "official", "authentic <band> merchandise", "licensed", anything implying the artist made, approved or profits.

Default tier ($129) avoids the question entirely: "a genuine used LP from your chosen decade, our pick". The Kit ($89/$99) has zero exposure: the customer's own record.

## 5. Pre-orders and deposits

FTC Mail, Internet or Telephone Order Merchandise Rule (16 CFR 435, https://www.ftc.gov/legal-library/browse/rules/mail-internet-or-telephone-order-merchandise-rule): any advertised ship date needs a reasonable basis; miss it and we send a delay notice offering a refund. The site already says "fully refundable before ship" and holds the ship date as a placeholder. Pick a date only once the sample run (about 3 weeks, sourcing/) proves the build, then add two weeks.

Florida sales tax: register with the Department of Revenue and get the annual resale certificate before the first ship (https://floridarevenue.com/taxes/taxesfees/Pages/annual_resale_certificate_sut.aspx).

## Product audit additions, 2026-11-11 evening (`../audits/AUDIT-product.md`)

1. **The 20 pre-sold units are already on a clock.** 16 CFR 435.2: ship within the time stated at sale, or within 30 days if no time was stated (https://www.law.cornell.edu/cfr/text/16/435.2). If those 20 buyers paid and were given no ship date, the 30 days started on the day they paid. Nothing can ship before the prototype tests pass, the cap alone is 2 to 3 weeks from order, and no cap quote exists. So a delay notice is almost certainly owed. A first delay of 30 days or less: send a notice with a new date and the right to cancel for a full refund; silence counts as agreeing. A longer or open-ended delay, or a second delay: the order is cancelled and refunded unless the buyer expressly agrees. Refunds within 7 working days (16 CFR 435.1). **Needs from Austin: who the 20 are, the date each paid, the amount, and what they were told about delivery.**
2. **Written warranty.** If a written warranty is offered on a product over $15 it needs the 16 CFR 701.3 disclosures, and implied warranties cannot then be disclaimed (15 USC 2308).
3. **"Made in USA."** Not available: the LEDs, adapter, dimmer and probably the cap are imported, and the standard is "all or virtually all" (16 CFR 323.2). "Assembled in Florida" or "Built in Florida from US and imported parts" is the honest form.
4. **Render labels.** The fictional labels "Ember" and "Meridian" in the renders: Ember Records was a real UK label. Check both before paid use. Every image in `../renders-zep/` shows a real label, band name, symbols and track list and must stay out of all marketing.
5. **Florida.** Only the general refund-policy statute was found (501.142, which exempts custom goods). No Florida pre-order or deposit statute was found; that is "not found", not "does not exist".
